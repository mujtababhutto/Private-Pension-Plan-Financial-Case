Retirement planning for a €250,000 investment portfolio.

A portfolio recommendation for a 45-year-old professor in Germany planning to retire at 68, developed around the household’s pension income, private healthcare costs and slightly risk-averse profile.

Executive summary

A diversified glide path designed around the retirement objective.

The assignment was to construct a retirement portfolio for a private client. The recommendation combines global equities, euro-denominated bonds, infrastructure and gold, with equity exposure reduced as retirement approaches.

Client profile

Long horizon, moderate risk capacity

Married with two children, a non-working spouse and an owned home. No portfolio liquidity is expected before retirement, and the client has a strong understanding of financial markets.

Portfolio strategy

Growth today, greater stability closer to retirement.

The recommended starting allocation combines 50% equities, 40% euro bonds and 10% gold. The equity allocation provides long-term growth, while bonds and gold support diversification and capital preservation.

Investment universe

Historical risk and return informed the asset selection.

The broader opportunity set includes global and emerging-market equities, listed property and infrastructure, euro bonds, inflation-linked exposure and gold. The recommended portfolio selects five complementary building blocks rather than simply choosing the assets with the highest historical returns.

Glide path allocation

The allocation becomes more conservative over time.

Equity exposure falls from 50% to 30% between ages 45 and 68. Bond exposure rises from 40% to 60%, while gold remains at 10% throughout.

Asset correlation analysis

Bonds and gold strengthen diversification.

Global equities and infrastructure provide the main growth exposure. Euro government bonds, corporate bonds and gold broaden the return drivers within the portfolio.

Monte Carlo simulation

Projected retirement capital remains above target in the downside case.

Across 50,000 simulated paths over 23 years, the median portfolio reaches €1.42 million before tax. The 10th-percentile outcome is €973,167, compared with the €675,070 retirement target.

Stress testing

Persistently weak real returns present the greatest funding risk.

Under the stagflation scenario—6% equity returns, 2% bond returns, 5% gold returns and an additional annual drag—the median retirement value falls to approximately €570,000.

Stagflation

The median outcome falls below the capital target, showing the impact of prolonged low real returns.

Sequence risk

A major drawdown close to retirement can materially reduce the capital available when withdrawals begin.

Longevity risk

A longer retirement period increases the total withdrawals required from the portfolio.

Policy risk

Changes to pension benefits, healthcare costs or investment taxation can affect the funding requirement.
